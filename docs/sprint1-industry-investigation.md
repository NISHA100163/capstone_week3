The three systems investigated are:

1. Bunnings
2. Kmart
3. JB Hi-Fi

## JB Hi-Fi Findings

The JB Hi-Fi investigation showed that a Product Catalogue may require both standard product information and commercial or promotional data.

Important findings include:

- Product ID/SKU and model number are important for product identification.
- Brand, category, product name and image should be core catalogue fields.
- Product variants such as colour, size or storage should be supported.
- Regular price and sale price may need to be stored separately.
- Discount information should be displayed when promotions are active.
- Ratings and reviews can support product comparison.
- Similar products can improve product discovery.
- Different categories may require different specifications.

# Data Classification

| Data Type | Examples | Explanation |
|---|---|---|
| Product / Source Data | Product name, brand, SKU, model, category, size, colour, price, image, specifications | Information obtained from the product source and used to describe the product. |
| User-Entered Data | Selected store, postcode, suburb, quantity | Information entered or selected by the customer. |
| System-Generated Data | Ratings, review count, related products, availability, discount display | Information calculated or generated by the system. |
| Stored Data | Product ID, product name, brand, category, SKU, price, description, image, created date, updated date | Information that should be stored in the database for future use. |

# Overall Findings

The industry investigation showed that Bunnings, Kmart and JB Hi-Fi all use a common set of core product data such as product name, brand, SKU, category, price and product image.

However, each system also provides additional features.

Bunnings focuses strongly on store availability, Click & Collect, delivery and product-specific features.

Kmart provides customer-focused features such as wishlist, stock notifications, fulfilment information and product visualisation.

JB Hi-Fi provides detailed model information, promotional pricing, discounts, product variants and similar-product recommendations.

Based on these findings, our Product Catalogue should use a common set of core product fields while allowing optional fields for availability, promotions, technical specifications and customer-focused features.

The system should also support different product categories because different products may require different information.
